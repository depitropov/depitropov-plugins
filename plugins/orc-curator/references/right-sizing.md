# Right-sizing

> **Shared policy.** `research` cites it, and `shape-tech` and `direction` follow it. It is policy
> only: no flow.

Ceremony that does not fit the change is the most frequent way to waste the time of the user. Two
rules carry almost all of it.

## Ceremony is set by the caller, never raised by the callee

A skill that is invoked at a reduced scope (one dimension and not six, a spot research and not a wide
one) **stays there**. Say which parts you skip and why, then skip them.

A light pass that grows in silence into a full interview is how "add social login" ends up as a stack
review and a study of competitors. When the scope that you got is really wrong for the work, **say so
in your relay, and let the caller or the user raise it.** Do not raise it yourself during the run.

The opposite is also true: never *lower* the ceremony that the caller asked for. When the user wants
the full treatment on something small, they get it.

**Too big for a light pass:** the change alters what the product is *for*, or who it is for. That
needs the full ceremony *and* a human who chooses to start it (`orc-curator:setup`). It is never an
automatic escalation from a one-line request.

## Research depth

Two dials, and the caller sets both:

- **mode**: `wide` when the options are really open; `deep` when the choice is made and you must know
  what it demands.
- **budget**: a number, of candidates or of sources.

Neither is a flag that the user types in normal use. The caller knows if the options are open; the
user often gets it wrong. A mode with no budget is the gap that lets a "quick look" use twenty
fetches.
